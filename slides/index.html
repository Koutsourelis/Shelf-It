Who it is for

Every morning, a department manager walks the aisles and checks every shelf by eye.

Shelf-It is for department managers and shelf workers in small and mid-size grocery stores that do not have fixed shelf cameras or sensors. All they need is a phone.

  • Gaps are noticed late.An empty spot sells nothing until someone walks past and sees it.
  • Stock waits in the backroom.The product is in the store, but nobody brought it out.
  • Orders go in from memory.What to reorder, and how much, depends on who is on shift.
Why it matters
40%

of the sales from an empty shelf are lost to the store. When a product is missing, 31% of shoppers buy it at another store and 9% do not buy it at all.

8.3%of products are missing from the shelf at any given moment, on average.
1 in 4gaps are products the store already has, just not on the shelf.
52 studiesin 29 countries behind these numbers.

Source: Gruen, Corsten & Bharadwaj (2002), Retail Out-of-Stocks: A Worldwide Examination of Extent, Causes and Consumer Responses. 52 studies in 29 countries; the 8.3% average comes from the 40 studies that measured it.

The solution

Take a photo. Get a fixed shelf.

Shelf-It is an AI agent with a small set of tools. It reads a photo of the shelf, compares it with the planogram, and turns every gap into work: a task for the shelf worker, or an order to the central warehouse.

model

The model reads.

Gemini turns the photo into a table: shelf, position, product, facings, and a box around each one.

code

Code computes.

Every count, unit, case and euro is computed by Python and SQL, and written to an audit trail.

person

People approve.

Orders above 3 cases or €50 wait for a yes from the manager, in the console or on Telegram.

Test shelf composed by code from real Greek product photos (sklavenitis.gr), not a photo of a real store. The three gaps are the ones in its planogram.

How it works

Five steps, from photo to refilled shelf.

01

Snap

person

One tap on the phone, or a phone left on the shelf as a live camera, checked every 5 seconds.

02

Read

model

Gemini turns the photo into a table: shelf, position, product, facings, box.

03

Check

model code

The shelf is compared with the planogram: missing, short, or on the wrong shelf.

04

Act

code

A task for the shelf worker, a Telegram alert, and an order to the central warehouse.

05

Refill

person

The worker brings the units in the task. With the live camera, the task closes only when a later frame shows the shelf fixed.

The tag shows who does each step. The model never decides a number.

What we built this weekend

Built in 36 hours.

  • Shelf visionOne structured Gemini call per photo, with boxes, and rejection of dark or covered frames
  • Live camera loopPhone as a shelf camera, mirrored to the laptop
  • Task enginePrioritised tasks with a crop of the spot; they close on rescan
  • Bounded orderingAutomatic orders within limits, approvals above them
  • Rules K1–K8Refill and layout rules in SQL, each with its source
  • Three shelf setsCoffee, drinks, and a real shelf with Open Food Facts data; detected from the photo
  • Evidence packsGreek trade press per product through treg
  • Shelf talkersPrintable signs with fal, after a person confirms name and price
  • Telegram botAlerts and approvals on the phone
  • ConsoleEvery step, tool call and figure, live and replayable
